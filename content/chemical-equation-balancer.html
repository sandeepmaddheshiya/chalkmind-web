How Chalkmind balances an equation

It follows the same reasoning a teacher would, so you can learn from it:

  1. Read the formulas from your handwriting, including subscripts and brackets like Ca(OH)₂.
  2. Count each element on both sides.
  3. Balance one element at a time, starting with one that appears in only one substance on each side, leaving hydrogen and oxygen until last.
  4. Treat polyatomic ions as units (like SO₄ or PO₄) when they appear unchanged on both sides.
  5. Recount and simplify to the smallest whole-number coefficients.

You see the steps and the final atom count, so it's clear why the answer is balanced.

Writing chemistry by hand: tips

  • Write subscripts clearly lower and smaller: H₂O, not H2O at the same height.
  • Use a clear arrow (→) between reactants and products.
  • Leave a space before each formula so coefficients have room.
  • Include state symbols, such as (aq), (s), (l) and (g), if your course expects them.
