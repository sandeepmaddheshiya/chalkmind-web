How it helps

Support your child without doing it for them

Learn the method together

Ask for the steps and go through them line by line. Each step can be explained in simpler language.

Hints, not answers

When your child is stuck, a hint points to the next step and leaves the thinking to them.

Find the real mistake

Check my work shows the first line that's wrong, so you can talk about the actual misunderstanding.

A homework routine that works

  1. Your child writes the problem and has a go on their own.
  2. If stuck for more than a few minutes, they ask for a hint.
  3. When finished, they use Check my work.
  4. For anything still unclear, they ask Explain and talk it through with you.

Why “I'll just show you how I learned it” can backfire

Many parents learned methods that differ from what's taught now, such as a different way to lay out long division, or a different method for fractions. Showing a different method isn't wrong, but it can confuse a child who is being marked on the method from class. Chalkmind explains the standard steps clearly, so you can learn the method your child is expected to use and help with that.

Questions to ask instead of giving the answer

  • “What is the question actually asking you to find?”
  • “What did you do in the example in your book?”
  • “Can you check your answer by putting it back in?”
  • “Which line are you least sure about?”
  • “Could you explain this step to me as if I were in your class?”

These keep your child doing the thinking. When neither of you is sure, that's the moment for a hint or the step-by-step explanation.

Setting it up for your child

  1. Install Chalkmind on the tablet your child uses for homework (Android 7.0 or later).
  2. Drawing, PDFs and simple maths work without an account. For AI help, sign in with a Google account you're comfortable with.
  3. Agree a routine: try first, hint when stuck, check before finishing.
  4. Look at the work together at the end. Ask your child to explain one problem to you.

Privacy and safety, plainly

  • There are no ads in the app and no tracking or analytics SDKs.
  • Boards and PDFs are saved on the device, not uploaded. When your child uses an AI feature, only what they select is sent to get the answer.
  • AI answers can occasionally be wrong. Every answer has a report button, and checking the answer by substitution is a good habit anyway.
  • Chalkmind is designed for ages 13 and up. Use it together with younger children.
