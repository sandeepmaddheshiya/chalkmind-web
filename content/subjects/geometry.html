Worked examples

Typical geometry questions, step by step

Missing angle in a triangle

A triangle has angles 47° and 68°. Find the third angle, x.

  1. Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
  2. x = 180° − 47° − 68° = 65°
Area of a compound shape

An L-shape is a 10 cm × 6 cm rectangle with a 4 cm × 3 cm rectangle cut from one corner.

  1. Whole rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 cm²
  2. Cut-out: 4 × 3 = 12 cm²
  3. Area: 60 − 12 = 48 cm²
Circle

A circle has radius 7 cm. Find its area and circumference.

  1. Area: πr² = π × 7² = 49π ≈ 153.9 cm²
  2. Circumference: 2πr = 14π ≈ 44.0 cm
Right-angled trigonometry

In a right triangle the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one angle is 30°. Find the side opposite the 30° angle.

  1. Use sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
  2. opposite = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm

Common geometry mistakes it helps you catch

  • Using Pythagoras on a triangle without a right angle. If the diagram has no right-angle mark, Pythagoras doesn't apply.
  • Mixing up the hypotenuse. It's always the side opposite the right angle, and the longest side.
  • Radius versus diameter. Using the diameter in πr² makes the area four times too big.
  • Forgetting square units. Areas are in cm² or m², not cm or m.
  • Calculator in radians. sin 30 in radian mode is about −0.99, not 0.5.
