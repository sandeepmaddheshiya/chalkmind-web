How it works

Solve the way you already write

Most maths apps make you photograph a page or type into a calculator. Chalkmind is a whiteboard first, so the problem, your working and the answer all stay on the same page.

1

Write the problem

Use the pen like paper: 2x + 6 = 14, a fraction sum, or a triangle with its sides labelled.

2

End it with “=”

Chalkmind notices the equals sign and works it out. To solve something specific, circle it with Select and tap Solve.

3

Get the answer in handwriting

Tap the answer and it’s written next to yours, on your line and at your slant. Tap Steps to put the working on the board.

More than arithmetic

Short calculations are solved instantly on the device. Anything that needs reading, like a geometry diagram, a word problem or a chemistry equation, goes to the AI, which reads the whole selection, including labels and right-angle marks.

  • Linear, simultaneous and quadratic equations
  • Expanding, factorising and algebraic identities
  • Pythagoras, angles, areas and other labelled diagrams
  • Physics formulas with units, and balancing chemical equations
  • Plot any equation as a graph you can pan and zoom, right beside the working

Learning, not copying

Built to help people understand the answer

An answer on its own doesn’t teach much. Every solution can be unpacked, and students can choose how much help they get.

Steps on the board

Each line of working can be placed under the problem, so it can be explained, annotated or erased line by line.

Hints instead of answers

Stuck? Ask for a hint. It points to the next step without solving the rest of the problem.

Check your own working

Write your solution and tap Check my work. Chalkmind finds the first line that’s wrong and explains why.

Getting the best results from handwriting

  • One problem at a time. Leave space between separate problems so they're read separately.
  • Clear symbols. Distinguish x from × (or use a dot for multiply), and make minus signs clearly horizontal.
  • Fractions with a clear bar. Write the numerator and denominator well above and below the line.
  • Label diagrams. Sides, angles, units and right-angle marks all help.
  • Use a stylus if you can. Neater strokes mean better recognition.

Answers that look like they belong

Most solvers show answers in a separate panel in a typed font. Chalkmind writes the answer on your board, following the line and slant of your own writing, so the page still reads like one continuous piece of work. That matters on a projector, where a sudden block of typed text interrupts the explanation, and in notes you'll revise from later. Longer answers, like a factorised expression or a balanced equation, can be placed as text instead.

Graphs beside the working

Write a function such as y = x² − 4 and plot it as a graph object on the board. Pan and zoom inside the graph to find intercepts and turning points, and keep it right next to the algebra that produced it.
