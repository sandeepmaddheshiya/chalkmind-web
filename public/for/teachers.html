A lesson with Chalkmind

From the starter to the exit ticket

Here's how a typical maths or science lesson can run from one Android tablet.

1

Starter

Ask the assistant for five quick questions on last lesson's topic and put them on the board.

2

Teach

Work through examples by hand. Rough shapes snap into neat diagrams; graphs plot right next to the working.

3

Worksheet

Open the PDF worksheet and solve the first question live with the class, writing straight on the page.

4

Misconceptions

Write a typical wrong answer and use Check my work to show the class exactly where it goes wrong.

5

Recap

Generate a mind map of the topic and build it out with the class's suggestions.

6

Share

Export the lesson's pages as a PDF for students who were absent or want to revise.

Better explanations

  • Answers written in handwriting that matches yours, so the board stays readable
  • Step-by-step working you can put on the board and talk through
  • Explain any word in a PDF in simple language, for students who need it
  • Laser pointer, dark chalkboard backgrounds and large pages for the back row
Honest limits. Chalkmind runs on Android only, and it isn't a shared classroom whiteboard yet: students can't draw on your board from their devices. It's built for teaching from the front or on a video call, and for students working on their own.

Using AI in lessons without replacing your teaching

  • Prepare with it. Drafting quizzes, worksheets and lesson plans is where AI saves the most time.
  • Explain yourself. Students learn from watching you reason; use solved answers to check, not to replace your explanation.
  • Show mistakes. Check my work on a typical wrong answer is a powerful way to discuss misconceptions.
  • Review before showing. Read generated material before it goes on the projector. AI can be wrong, and every answer can be reported.

Teaching beyond maths

Science teachers balance equations and work through physics calculations on the board. Language and humanities teachers use PDFs with in-context word look-up, mind maps for essay planning and timelines, and quizzes on a text. Any teacher can turn a lesson into a revision PDF.

Questions

How do I show Chalkmind on a projector or TV?

Connect the tablet with a USB-C to HDMI adapter (many Samsung tablets support this), cast the screen to a Chromecast or smart TV, or share your screen in Google Meet or Zoom for online classes.

Do students need accounts?

No. You teach from your own device. Students only need the app if they want to use it themselves.

Does it store student data?

No student data is collected. Your boards are saved on your device, and the AI only receives what you select.

Can students work on the same board at the same time?

Not yet. Chalkmind is designed for one person writing, which suits teaching from the front or on a call.

Is there a school or class plan?

Not yet. If you'd like to use Chalkmind across a school, email support@chalkmind.tech; we'd like to hear what you need.

Can I prepare lessons at home and teach from them in class?

Yes. Boards are saved on the tablet with all their pages, so you can prepare pages and PDFs in advance and open them in class.
