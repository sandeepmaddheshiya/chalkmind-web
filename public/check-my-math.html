Example

A typical slip, caught

A student solves 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5 and gets x = 7. The final answer is wrong, but the real problem is earlier:

Student's working
  1. 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5
  2. 3x − 2 = 2x + 5 ← first mistake
  3. x = 7
What Chalkmind says

The first mistake is on line 2. When you expand 3(x − 2), the 3 multiplies both terms, so it's 3x − 6. Then 3x − 6 = 2x + 5 gives x = 11. Check by substituting: 3(11 − 2) = 27 and 2(11) + 5 = 27. ✓

Because it names the exact step, the student learns the rule they broke (distribute to every term) instead of just copying a corrected answer.

Why the first mistake

One clear correction beats a page of red ink

When one early line is wrong, every line after it is “wrong” too, even when the method is right. Pointing at the first error tells you what actually needs fixing.

Sign and bracket slips

Dropped negatives, half-expanded brackets and terms moved across the equals sign without changing sign.

Arithmetic errors

A wrong subtraction on line 2 that quietly ruins a correct method. Chalkmind says which number is off.

Method gaps

Dividing only one term by a number, or squaring a sum term by term. It explains the rule, not just the fix.

How to use it

  1. Write your full working, one step per line.
  2. Use Select to circle the problem and your working.
  3. Open the AI Assistant and tap Check my work.
  4. Read the explanation, fix the line, and check again if you like.

More examples of mistakes it explains

Fractions

Working: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5

Feedback: you can't add the tops and bottoms separately. Use a common denominator: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Indices

Working: x³ × x⁴ = x¹²

Feedback: when multiplying powers of the same base, add the indices: x³ × x⁴ = x⁷. Multiplying them applies to (x³)⁴ = x¹².

Physics units

Working: a car travels 90 km in 45 minutes, so speed = 90 ÷ 45 = 2 km/h.

Feedback: 45 minutes is 0.75 hours. Speed = 90 ÷ 0.75 = 120 km/h (or 2 km per minute).

Tips for the clearest feedback

  • Write one step per line, so each line can be checked against the one before.
  • Include the question in your selection, so the check knows what you were solving.
  • Don't erase wrong attempts if you want to know why they were wrong; select them too.
  • After fixing a mistake, check again: a second mistake later in the working will then show up.

Accuracy

Check my work is powered by AI, which can occasionally misread handwriting or make an error. The explanation always says what it read and why it thinks a line is wrong, so you can judge for yourself. Substituting your final answer back into the question is a good second check, and you can report any wrong feedback in the app.

Questions

Does it just tell me the right answer?

It tells you where your working first goes wrong and why, then shows how that step should look. You can ask for a hint instead if you want to fix the rest yourself.

Can it check working it can't read?

If a line is hard to read, the explanation says what it read so you can rewrite it. Clear, separate lines give the best results.

Which subjects can it check?

Algebra, arithmetic, geometry, physics calculations and chemistry, such as balancing equations or mole calculations.

Can teachers use it to mark work?

Teachers use it on the board to show a class where a typical solution goes wrong. It's not a marking system and doesn't store student results.

Is checking work free?

Check my work is an AI feature. It needs a free sign-in and counts towards the daily free AI answers; Chalkmind Plus gives you many more.

Will it check every line, or just the answer?

Every line. It looks for the first line that doesn't follow from the one before, which is usually where the real misunderstanding is.
