How Chalkmind balances an equation

It follows the same reasoning a teacher would, so you can learn from it:

  1. Read the formulas from your handwriting, including subscripts and brackets like Ca(OH)₂.
  2. Count each element on both sides.
  3. Balance one element at a time, starting with one that appears in only one substance on each side, leaving hydrogen and oxygen until last.
  4. Treat polyatomic ions as units (like SO₄ or PO₄) when they appear unchanged on both sides.
  5. Recount and simplify to the smallest whole-number coefficients.

You see the steps and the final atom count, so it's clear why the answer is balanced.

Writing chemistry by hand: tips

  • Write subscripts clearly lower and smaller: H₂O, not H2O at the same height.
  • Use a clear arrow (→) between reactants and products.
  • Leave a space before each formula so coefficients have room.
  • Include state symbols, such as (aq), (s), (l) and (g), if your course expects them.

Questions

Which equations can it balance?

Combustion, synthesis, decomposition, displacement and neutralisation reactions, including ones with polyatomic ions such as sulfate or phosphate.

Does it explain how it balanced the equation?

Yes. It shows the steps and the atom count for each element, so you can follow the method and do the next one yourself.

Can it do other chemistry?

It also helps with molar mass, mole and concentration calculations, pH questions and predicting products of common reactions.

How do I write subscripts by hand?

Write the small number slightly lower and smaller after the element, as you would on paper, for example H₂O. Chalkmind reads it from the handwriting.

Can it balance redox and ionic equations?

It can help with half-equations and ionic equations, including charges. For complex redox reactions, check the result against your course's method, as you would with any tool.
