The best apps for handwritten notes on an Android tablet

Many students find they remember handwritten notes better than typed ones, and a tablet makes them searchable, shareable and impossible to lose in a bag. Here's how to pick the right app for study notes, revision and lecture slides.

The best notes app is the one you'll keep using all term. That usually comes down to three things: whether writing feels natural, whether you can find things again, and whether the app helps you use your notes to revise, not just store them.

What matters in a handwritten notes app

  • Writing feel: low lag, smooth strokes, palm rejection, and pens you can switch quickly. Test this first; nothing else matters if writing feels wrong.
  • Paper styles: lined, grid (for maths and science) and plain pages, plus an endless canvas for mind maps.
  • PDF import: annotating lecture slides, worksheets and textbook chapters is a huge part of studying.
  • Organisation: notebooks or folders per subject, and clear names, so a term's notes don't become one long scroll.
  • Search and conversion: some apps can search your handwriting or convert it to typed text, which is useful for long courses.
  • Revision tools: turning notes into flashcards, quizzes or summaries is where a modern app can save hours.
  • Export and backup: you should be able to get your notes out as PDFs, and know where they're stored.
  • Subject help: for maths and science, equation and graph tools, or help solving and checking work.

The apps

Samsung Notes: the default choice on a Galaxy tablet

Preinstalled on Samsung devices and built for the S Pen. It imports PDFs so you can write on them, converts handwriting to text, and can straighten handwriting. It's free and polished for plain writing.

Best for: anyone with a Galaxy tablet who wants reliable everyday notes. Less good for: revision tools and subject help; it's only on Samsung devices.

Microsoft OneNote: best organisation

Free with a Microsoft account, with notebooks, sections and pages that scale well over a long course, and syncing across devices.

Best for: students who want everything organised and synced, especially at schools that use Microsoft 365. Less good for: the handwriting experience on Android is more limited than on Windows, and its Math Assistant is documented for Windows and iPad.

MyScript Notes (formerly Nebo): best handwriting recognition

Known for turning handwriting into typed, editable text and diagrams very accurately. It's free to try with paid features, and works on a subset of Android devices with active pens.

Best for: students who want typed notes in the end but prefer to write. Less good for: check that your specific tablet is supported before paying.

Squid: a long-running Android-native notes app

A handwriting and PDF markup app built for Android, with pen, finger and stylus support. Some features, including PDF import, have been part of its Premium subscription; check the current plan in Google Play.

Best for: straightforward handwritten notebooks and PDF markup on Android. Less good for: revision and subject-specific help.

Google Keep: best for quick notes, not study notes

Great for lists and quick reminders with simple drawings, synced to your Google account. It isn't designed for page after page of lecture notes or PDF annotation.

Questions

Is it better to take notes by hand or by typing?

Writing by hand can help learning because it pushes you to summarise in your own words rather than copy everything down, though how you review your notes matters more than how you take them. A tablet gives you that benefit plus easy organisation, backups and PDFs.

What's the best free handwritten notes app for Android?

On a Samsung Galaxy tablet, Samsung Notes is free and excellent for writing. OneNote is free with a Microsoft account. Chalkmind's whiteboard and notes are free, with AI study tools limited to a daily number of answers on the free plan.

Which app converts handwriting to text?

Samsung Notes (with the S Pen) and MyScript Notes (formerly Nebo) are known for converting handwriting to typed text. Chalkmind doesn't convert notes to typed text; it focuses on solving, explaining and revising from them.

Do I need an expensive tablet?

No. Any Android tablet with a decent stylus works for notes. A tablet with an active stylus (such as a Galaxy Tab with an S Pen) gives the most paper-like writing.
