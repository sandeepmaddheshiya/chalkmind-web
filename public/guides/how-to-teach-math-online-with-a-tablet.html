How to teach maths online, or on a projector, with a tablet

What you need, how to connect it, and the board habits that make handwritten maths easy to follow on a shared screen, whether you're in a classroom or on a video call.

3 min read · Updated 17 November 2026

Handwritten maths is hard to replace. Typing equations is slow, and drawing with a mouse is worse. A tablet with a stylus gives you the speed of a real whiteboard, plus things a whiteboard can't do: unlimited pages, PDFs, saved notes and, with the right app, answers and explanations on demand.

What you need

ItemWhy it matters
An Android tablet, 10″ or larger if possibleMore room to write and more readable when mirrored to a big screen.
A stylusNeater handwriting and palm rejection. Some tablets include one (for example Samsung Galaxy Tab S models with the S Pen).
A way to show the screenA USB-C to HDMI adapter, a Chromecast or casting-capable TV, or a video-call app for online lessons.
ChargingScreen sharing and an always-on display use a lot of battery; keep a charger nearby for long sessions.

Connecting to a projector or TV

  • Wired (most reliable): a USB-C to HDMI adapter into the projector or TV. Not every tablet supports video over USB-C, so check your model's specifications first. Many Samsung tablets do, and can also run Samsung DeX.
  • Wireless: cast your screen to a Chromecast or a smart TV from the quick settings panel. It's convenient, but there can be a small delay and it depends on a good Wi-Fi network.
  • Test before the lesson: check that small handwriting is readable from the back of the room. If it isn't, zoom in, use a thicker pen and write bigger.

Teaching online with Meet or Zoom

  1. Join the call from the tablet's video-call app (Google Meet and Zoom both have Android apps).
  2. Choose the option to share your screen and confirm.
  3. Switch to your whiteboard app. Students see the board; you see your full screen.
  4. Use a laser pointer or circle things as you talk, since students can't see where you're looking.

Tip: turn on Do Not Disturb before sharing your screen, so messages and notifications don't appear in front of the class.

Board habits that make maths easy to follow

  • One step per line, aligned on the equals sign. Students follow the logic downwards, just like on paper.
  • Write bigger than feels natural. What looks fine on a tablet is small on a projector or a phone screen.
  • Use colour with purpose. One colour for working, another for the key step or answer, and a highlighter for what to remember.
  • A new page for each idea. Pages are free, and clean pages are easier to export and revise from later.
  • Dark backgrounds for projectors. A dark chalkboard-style page reduces glare in bright rooms.
  • Pause after each line. Let students copy or think before you move on. Screen sharing can lag behind your pen by a moment.

Working from worksheets and textbooks

Import the worksheet or chapter as a PDF and write directly on it. Solve the first question together, leave space for students to try the next, and come back to annotate common mistakes. When a word in the text needs explaining, a whiteboard that can look up words in context saves a detour.

After the lesson

  • Export the board as a PDF and share it with the class, especially students who were absent.
  • Keep the file for next year. Lessons on a tablet are reusable in a way a wiped whiteboard isn't.
  • Note which examples caused trouble and add a slide of “common mistakes” for next time.

Where AI helps (and where it doesn't)

AI is useful for the preparation around a lesson: drafting quiz questions, a worksheet at a few difficulty levels, a mind map to start a topic, or checking where a typical wrong answer goes off track. It's less useful as a replacement for explaining: students learn from watching you reason through a problem. Use AI answers as a draft you check, not as the lesson itself.

Questions

Do I need a stylus to teach maths on a tablet?

Not strictly, but it makes a big difference. Writing with a finger is slower and harder to read; a stylus makes handwriting look much like it does on paper.

How do I connect an Android tablet to a projector?

Use a USB-C to HDMI adapter if your tablet supports video output (many Samsung Galaxy Tab models do), or cast to a Chromecast or a TV that supports screen casting.

Can students see my tablet screen in Google Meet?

Yes. In the Meet app on Android, use the option to share your screen, then switch to your whiteboard app.

Is a tablet better than a graphics tablet plugged into a laptop?

A tablet with a stylus lets you see where you're writing on the screen itself, which most people find much easier for maths than writing on a pad while looking at a monitor.
